Data

The data behind 3D ICE

Every layer comes from a published dataset, model or preprint. The code is open source under the MIT License on GitHub; the data stay under their producers’ licences and terms, listed in DATA_LICENSES.md.

Terrain

  • BedMachine Antarctica v4
  • Bedmap3 v1.0 Antarctica
  • BedMachine Greenland v6
  • QRF subglacial topography Greenland, Palmer et al. (2025)

Ice flow

  • MEaSUREs phase-based velocity Antarctica
  • ITS_LIVE V02.1 summary velocity mosaic, 2014–2024 Greenland; generated using auto-RIFT (Gardner et al., 2018) and provided by NASA MEaSUREs ITS_LIVE (Gardner et al., 2025)

Beneath the ice

  • Antarctic Ice Sheet Ensemble Friction Inversion (AISEFI): Basal Shear Stress with Uncertainties Jager (2026) · preprint
  • Greenland Ice Sheet Ensemble Friction Inversion (GrISEFI): Basal Shear Stress with Uncertainties Jager (2026) · preprint
  • Subglacial hydrology Antarctica, Ehrenfeucht et al. (2025) · data

Ocean and sea ice

  • WAOM2 ocean circulation Antarctica, Boeira Dias et al. (2023)
  • Arctic Ocean Physics Analysis Greenland; generated using E.U. Copernicus Marine Service Information
  • Ice-shelf basal melt Antarctica, Galton-Fenzi et al. (2025) · data
  • NOAA/NSIDC sea-ice concentration v6 Meier et al. (2026)

Change and the future

  • CPOM elevation change v1.0 Antarctica; ERS-2, Envisat and CryoSat-2 · Shepherd et al. (2019)
  • ISMIP6 projections to 2300 Antarctica, Seroussi et al. (2024)

Rebound

  • Paxman, Austermann & Hollyday (2022) grid files v3
  • Le Meur & Huybrechts (1996) idealised ELRA and Airy models

Basins

  • MEaSUREs Antarctic Boundaries v2
  • Greenland basins Mouginot & Rignot (2019)
  • Greenland outlet catchments Mankoff et al. (2020)

Places

  • Stations: COMNAP (educational, non-commercial use) · INTERACT
  • Names: SCAR Gazetteer · NunaGIS · GEBCO · Natural Earth
  • Ice cores: NOAA NCEI · Davidge et al. (2022)

What’s new

All updates

  1. November 13, 2026 Research edition

    Greenland’s outlet catchments

    A second set of Greenland basins: the outlet catchments of Mankoff et al. (2020), the ice that drains to each outlet at the margin of the ice sheet or of a glacier around it, with water routed at ice-overburden pressure. Lines divide the 3,126 catchments of at least 10 km², 97% of the ice area. A Basin set chooser under Basins switches between them and Mouginot & Rignot’s seven regions, which stay the default.

  2. November 3, 2026 Both editions

    The grounding line

    A dark line now marks where grounded ice meets floating ice or the ocean, traced cell for cell from the terrain on screen. While the 2300 projection plays, it is traced again from each frame and retreats with the ice, and the tour’s stop at the ice shelves draws it.

  3. November 2, 2026 Both editions

    Switch between editions

    A Public | Research switch beside the language switch opens the other edition in the same language and at the same region. The public edition’s panel takes the research edition’s look, with each legend under its layer.

  4. November 1, 2026 Research edition Version 0.4.0

    Observed sea ice, 1978 to 2026

    Monthly sea-ice concentration around Antarctica and Greenland from the NOAA/NSIDC climate data record, from November 1978 to September 2026. The readout gives the month’s extent and its rank among the years, and an orange line marks the 1981–2010 median ice edge.

  5. November 1, 2026 Research edition Version 0.4.0

    Antarctic elevation change since 1995

    CPOM’s record of surface elevation change from ERS-2, Envisat and CryoSat-2 radar altimetry, played from 1995 to 2026 or shown as a rate, with a chart of the mass change of each of 18 drainage basins.

  6. November 1, 2026 Research edition Version 0.4.0

    Ice-core sites

    54 drilling sites, from Vostok and EPICA Dome C to Camp Century and EastGRIP. Each site’s card gives the core’s depth, the span of its record and a link to its publication.

  7. November 1, 2026 Research edition Version 0.4.0

    A rebuilt research panel

    One column of collapsible groups, with each legend under its layer and one time feature at a time. With nothing switched on, the panel is less than half as tall as before.

  8. October 22, 2026 Both editions Version 0.3.1

    Corrections from an audit

    The Antarctic ocean currents now follow the modelled flow, the exaggeration slider shows the true factor, the subglacial channels the layer used to drop are back, and sea-level equivalents use freshwater density.

  9. October 6, 2026 Both editions Version 0.3.0

    Antarctica’s ice to 2300

    A year slider plays the ice sheet from 2015 to 2300 under low emissions, high emissions, and high emissions with ice-shelf collapse: the mean change of eight ISMIP6 models (Seroussi et al., 2024), with their sea-level contribution and its range. A tour stop plays the high-emission future in fifteen seconds.

  10. September 30, 2026 Public edition

    The public edition, with a guided tour

    A second edition for visitors, classrooms and outreach stands, in English and Chinese, with a guided tour and a plain-language note beside every layer. The research edition keeps every dataset at its usual address.

  11. September 22, 2026 Both editions

    The ice-free rebound

    Watch the bed rise once the ice is gone, and see which land below sea level today would emerge, from the published response of Paxman et al. (2022). The research edition adds idealised flexure and Airy models.

  12. August 13, 2026 Both editions

    Polar places and features

    Research stations and geographic names in one search, for both ice sheets. The research edition adds the refined drainage basins.

Every change, with its details, is in the changelog.
